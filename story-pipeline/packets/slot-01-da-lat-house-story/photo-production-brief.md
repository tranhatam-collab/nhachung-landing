## Overview

This photo set supports the primary Đà Lạt house story — the founding property and proof-of-concept for the Nhà Chung model. The images must communicate: authentic community, purposeful work, and a home that enables both.

**Do not produce stock-photo-style imagery.** Every photo must feel real — imperfect light, genuine expression, real moments. Staged smiles and clean-desk setups are disqualifying.

---

## Required Photos — 5 Total

---

### Photo 1: Hero Shot

**Purpose:** Cover image for the story article, social media lead image, and landing page feature.

**Subject:** Exterior facade or primary living/social space of the Đà Lạt house, ideally showing architectural character (pitched roof, garden, wooden elements, or mountain backdrop).

**Composition guide:**
- Horizontal (landscape) orientation required
- Subject fills 60–70% of frame — do not center-crop to just the building
- Include some environmental context: garden, street, mountain mist, or neighboring greenery
- If interior: shoot from a doorway or wide corner to show the full room — people optional but welcome

**Lighting requirements:**
- Golden hour strongly preferred: 30 minutes before sunset or 30–45 minutes after sunrise
- Warm, directional light that emphasizes texture (wood grain, tile, stone)
- If overcast: acceptable only if mist adds atmosphere (Đà Lạt mist is an asset — use it)
- Avoid: harsh midday light, blown-out windows, flat fluorescent interior lighting

**What to avoid:**
- Cars, motorbikes, or power lines dominating the frame (remove or recompose)
- Garbage bins, construction materials, or anything visually distracting in foreground
- Members posing formally in front of the building
- HDR processing or heavy saturation

**File specs:**
- Minimum resolution: 2400 x 1600 px (landscape)
- Color profile: sRGB
- Format: JPEG, maximum 8 MB per file
- Deliver one final edit + one unedited RAW or DNG

**Caption format:**
> "The Đà Lạt house — [brief honest description of what is shown], [month + year]."
> Example: "The Đà Lạt house — living room at dusk, with mountain view visible through the open door, March 2026."

---

### Photo 2: Community Dinner

**Purpose:** Core story image. Shows the social heartbeat of the house. Used in story body, Instagram, and investor materials.

**Subject:** 4–6 members gathered around the dining table during an actual meal — not a styled "dinner party" setup.

**Composition guide:**
- Shoot from one end of the table or from a slight diagonal angle — not directly overhead
- Frame should include faces (even if partially), food on the table, and hands (eating, passing, gesturing)
- Allow for some natural clutter: cups, phones set down, napkins — this signals authenticity
- Laughter or mid-conversation expression is ideal; not everyone needs to look at camera

**Lighting requirements:**
- Natural light from windows is ideal (evening dinners near a window, or weekend lunch)
- Warm overhead pendant light is acceptable if it creates a cohesive mood — avoid mixing warm and cool sources
- No flash. If light is insufficient, use a fast lens (f/1.8–f/2.8) and raise ISO rather than flash

**What to avoid:**
- Everyone looking at the camera at the same time (staged)
- Empty plates or a table that looks pre-set for a photoshoot
- A single person isolated from the group
- Faces cropped out — at minimum 2 full faces visible

**File specs:**
- Minimum resolution: 2400 x 1600 px
- Color profile: sRGB
- Format: JPEG, maximum 8 MB
- Deliver one final edit + one unedited RAW or DNG

**Caption format:**
> "Dinner at Nhà Chung Đà Lạt — [what is happening in the moment], [month + year]."
> Example: "Dinner at Nhà Chung Đà Lạt — members sharing a hotpot after a long work week, April 2026."

---

### Photo 3: Work Session

**Purpose:** Shows that Nhà Chung is a serious work environment, not just a hostel with good vibes. Used in story, LinkedIn, and product landing page.

**Subject:** One member working at their desk or on their laptop in a house common space or their private room. The setup should look like real work — not a "working from cafe" lifestyle shot.

**Composition guide:**
- Member should be genuinely working or reading — not looking at camera
- Frame to include the work surface, the person from approximately mid-torso up, and ideally a window or natural light source
- Environmental details welcome: coffee cup, notebook, secondary screen, headphones on desk — not held up for camera
- Portrait or landscape both acceptable for this shot

**Lighting requirements:**
- Natural window light is required — side light from a window creates depth and avoids the flat "home office" look
- Avoid overhead fluorescent or LED strips as the primary light source
- If shooting in the member's room: open the curtains and position the subject near the window, slightly angled

**What to avoid:**
- Member looking at camera (this is the one shot that should be almost entirely candid)
- Clean desk with no sign of real work (empty notebooks, just a laptop on a white table)
- Excessive depth-of-field blur that hides the environment entirely
- Screen visible and showing personal information

**File specs:**
- Minimum resolution: 2400 x 1600 px
- Color profile: sRGB
- Format: JPEG, maximum 8 MB
- Deliver one final edit + one unedited RAW or DNG

**Caption format:**
> "Deep work at Nhà Chung — [member's first name or role], [location in house], [month + year]."
> Example: "Deep work at Nhà Chung — Linh, a UX designer, in the second-floor reading nook, March 2026."

---

### Photo 4: Contribution Moment

**Purpose:** The heart of the Nhà Chung model — members contributing to shared life. This image must tell the story of reciprocity without a caption.

**Subject:** A member actively doing something for the house or for other members — cooking a meal for everyone, fixing something, teaching a skill, watering the garden, or leading a house workshop.

**Composition guide:**
- Subject mid-action — hands doing something, body engaged
- Include context: kitchen, workshop tools, a whiteboard, a garden — the environment tells the story
- Other members can be in the background (naturally blurred or partially in frame) to suggest audience/benefit
- Expression should be focused or satisfied — not looking at camera

**Lighting requirements:**
- Ambient light preferred — kitchen overhead lighting is fine for cooking shots if warm-toned
- For outdoor contribution shots (garden, repair work): overcast or shade preferred to avoid harsh shadows on face
- Avoid flash in all contribution shots

**What to avoid:**
- The member looking proud-at-camera while holding the thing they made — this reads as marketing
- Empty kitchen or workspace with no evidence of the activity
- Multiple people doing the exact same task (feels staged)
- Overly tight crop that loses the activity context

**File specs:**
- Minimum resolution: 2400 x 1600 px
- Color profile: sRGB
- Format: JPEG, maximum 8 MB
- Deliver one final edit + one unedited RAW or DNG

**Caption format:**
> "[Member first name] [verb + what they are doing] — [brief context], [month + year]."
> Example: "Minh cooking Sunday dinner for the house — a weekly tradition started in Month 1, March 2026."

### Photo 5: Member Portrait

**Purpose:** Human face of the story. Paired with the member's quote or mini-profile in the story body. Used on story page and social media.

**Subject:** One member in their private room or their favorite house spot (balcony, reading nook, garden corner). Honest expression — not a professional headshot, not a selfie.

**Composition guide:**
- Portrait orientation preferred (for story card use)
- Member should be relaxed — seated, leaning on something, or in a natural posture they actually use
- Include some of their personal environment: a book they're reading, their plants, a poster, their shoes — detail that makes them real
- Eyes can be looking at camera or slightly off — both work if the expression is genuine

**Lighting requirements:**
- Window light strongly preferred — position member so window light falls on one side of face (not directly front-lit)
- Golden hour portrait is excellent if the member has an outdoor spot they love
- If room is dark: use a reflector (white card or foam board) to bounce window light — do not use flash

**What to avoid:**
- Formal smile or "passport photo" stiffness
- Plain white wall as the only background
- Heavy retouching (skin smoothing, eye brightening) — this is a community story, not a brand campaign
- Cluttered background that distracts from the subject

**File specs:**
- Minimum resolution: 1600 x 2400 px (portrait) or 2400 x 1600 px (landscape)
- Color profile: sRGB
- Format: JPEG, maximum 8 MB
- Deliver one final edit + one unedited RAW or DNG

**Caption format:**
> "[Member first name], [role/profession], in [location in house], [month + year]."
> Example: "Hoa, a freelance illustrator from Hà Nội, on the rooftop garden she started in her first week, April 2026."

## Day-of-Shoot Checklist

**Before leaving for the shoot:**
- [ ] Confirm shoot date/time with photographer (minimum 72h before)
- [ ] Confirm 3–4 member participants are available and aware of the brief
- [ ] Share this brief with photographer at least 48h before shoot
- [ ] Confirm all 5 members appearing have signed the consent form (see below)
- [ ] Charge camera batteries and format memory cards
- [ ] Pack reflector (white foam board minimum), extra memory card
- [ ] Check Đà Lạt weather — reschedule if full day of heavy rain (mist is fine, downpour is not)

**Morning of shoot:**
- [ ] Brief all members on what we are capturing — emphasize candid and authentic, not posed
- [ ] Walk through the house with photographer to identify best light sources and spots
- [ ] Note golden hour times (check a weather app) — schedule Hero Shot and Portrait around these windows
- [ ] Clear any visually distracting items from key areas (not staging — just tidying)

**During shoot:**
- [ ] Shoot Hero Shot first (golden hour window is fixed)
- [ ] Allow at least 45 minutes for Community Dinner shot — wait for a real moment
- [ ] Check photos on a laptop or large screen before photographer leaves — verify: no missed focus, no blocked faces, no accidental screen content visible
- [ ] Capture at minimum 3x the required photos for each shot type (select best in post)

**End of shoot:**
- [ ] Back up all files to two locations before photographer leaves the house
- [ ] Complete the handoff notes (see section below)
- [ ] Confirm file delivery timeline with photographer (standard: edited finals within 5 business days)
